My dissertation asks how extreme small body size shapes the behavioral and
cognitive processes of spiders. As animals miniaturize, their nervous systems
face real physical constraints, yet many tiny spiders perform sophisticated
behaviors. I test how the relationship between brain size and body size relates
to cognitive performance, such as prey-capture efficiency in *Pholcus
phalangioides* cellar spiders.

This work combines behavioral experiments with quantitative neuroanatomy: I use
microCT scanning and 3D image analysis to measure brain and body structures, and
pair them with tasks like web-navigation memory in *Frontinella communis*. The
goal is a clearer picture of how cognition is built, and constrained, at very
small scales.
